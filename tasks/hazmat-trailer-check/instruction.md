Heartland Freight Lines' dispatch team checks every hazardous materials trailer before it leaves the dock. For each trailer, the team needs the exact placards it must display and a complete list of any pairs of packages that are loaded together illegally. Some of the packages are Heartland's own house blends, which Heartland offers for transportation itself. The sole governing authority is the Hazardous Materials Regulations, 49 CFR Parts 171 to 180, as in force on October 1, 2026; no other rules, standards, guidance, or versions of the regulations apply.

The shipment data is in /app/data/trailers.json. This file contains one record for each trailer and the packages carried on that trailer. For every package other than a house blend, the record gives its UN number, shipping name, hazard class, and subsidiary hazards, together with its packing group, physical state, gross weight in pounds, packaging type, maximum capacity in liters, maximum net mass in kilograms, and, for gas receptacles, water capacity in pounds. It also identifies whether the package is a limited quantity and whether it contains residue only.

Except for house blends, each package record also supplies the labels the package must bear. An empty label list means that no package labels are required; it does not mean that label information is missing. The record includes the poison inhalation hazard zone and radioactive label, where applicable, as well as the explosives compatibility group and whether an explosive is an article or a substance. Other fields identify fireworks, cyanide, and acid. The loading information identifies the facility where the package was loaded and the bay it occupies. Package and trailer identifiers in the input are the identifiers to use in the result.

A package with lab data is a house blend. None of the house blends is named in the § 172.101 table, and their records give no UN number, shipping name, hazard class, packing group or labels, so each one must be classified from its lab data. Every temperature, pressure, vapor pressure and corrosion rate in the lab data states its own unit. The lab data is the only information on the blends; there is no human experience data and no PHMSA determination for any of them. No blend is a hazardous substance, a hazardous waste or a marine pollutant, and a blend is a pesticide only when its record says so. Every subsidiary hazard of a house blend requires a subsidiary label. Heartland reclasses as a combustible liquid every flammable liquid that the regulations allow it to reclass. For a mixture, the lab reports each toxic component's mole fraction and its own vapor pressure, and no activity coefficients are known. Where the lab reports only a four-hour LC50, Heartland uses the conversion the regulation allows, and it determines the LC50 of a gas mixture with the formula in § 173.133(b)(1)(i). The skin test gives the shortest exposure that caused irreversible damage and when the damage was seen, and the other findings are the lab's results for Divisions 4.1, 4.2, 4.3 and 5.1 with their packing groups.

The carrier information is in /app/data/carrier.json. It identifies the carrier, establishes domestic highway transport, and describes the trailer layout. Each trailer has ten floor bays, numbered in order from the nose toward the doors, with all packages carried in a single tier. Every trailer is one transport vehicle. All movements are domestic and by highway only.

Any cyanide and any acid identified in the data would generate hydrogen cyanide if mixed. Materials of the same class in the data cannot react dangerously with one another.
