Borinquen Site Engineers audits construction sites in Puerto Rico for their owners. The owners want to know every requirement of their stormwater permit that was missed or met late between January 1 and September 30, 2026. Every site is covered by EPA's 2022 Construction General Permit as modified on April 8, 2025, which is the only governing rule; no other law, guidance or local requirement applies.

The records are in /app/data/. sites.json gives each site's inspection schedule under Part 4.2, its normal working hours on business days, its receiving waters (with the parameters each is listed as impaired for and its antidegradation tier) and its areas, each with the acres it disturbs, the water it discharges to, and work_completed_on, the day its construction activities ended for good (null if still under way). rain_gauge.json holds the daily readings of each site's rain gauge in inches; days without measurable rain are not listed. construction_log.json lists every day and area with construction activities. stabilization_records.json lists each stabilization of an area with the times it was initiated and completed. inspections.json lists each site inspection with its completion time, the areas inspected and the time its report was completed. findings.json lists each condition found, with the control, location and fix, what the condition needed, whether the inspection report documents that routine maintenance could not be completed by the close of the next business day, the completion time, the corrective action log entries, and whether the SWPPP needed a change and when it was updated. trackout.json lists sediment tracked onto paved roads, with the times it occurred and was removed. A null time means it never happened.

Each site has one operator. Every activity in the log is an earth-disturbing activity, construction activities took place on exactly the days logged, which were the days planned, and nothing else, including inspections, stabilization and maintenance, was a construction activity. Work in an area ceases on its last logged day before a break, a break being the calendar days between two logged days, and an area with work still under way kept working after September 30 without a break long enough to require stabilization. Each listed impairment meets the permit's definition of an impaired water. No site is arid, semi-arid or drought-stricken or a linear construction site or discharges within Lands of Exclusive Federal Jurisdiction, none had snow, frozen conditions, dewatering or unforeseen circumstances, EPA required nothing, no report documents that a repeated fix should stay routine maintenance, and no record documents that a corrective action could not be completed by its deadline. Every site uses the reduced inspection frequency for stabilized areas wherever the permit allows. Findings with the same control, location and fix are the same fix to the same control at the same location, and the fourth and later occurrences of a fix are handled as corrective actions.

The office applies the permit's 24-hour periods to each calendar day's gauge reading, treats that rain as having fallen at 23:59 that day, and treats a run of consecutive days with measurable rain as one storm, needing an inspection after the first and after the last of its days whose rain reaches the permit's threshold. An inspection covers every area it lists and counts for every inspection of those areas then required. An area's inspection requirements begin on its first logged day, and the next inspection is due within the schedule's interval counted from the date of the area's latest inspection, or its first logged day: an area on a seven-day schedule inspected on May 5 is due again by the end of May 12, and if it is next inspected on May 14, that requirement was met late and the next is due by the end of May 21. When an area's schedule changes because its stabilization is completed, its first month after stabilization ends or work in it resumes, the next inspection is due within the new interval counted from the later of its latest inspection and that day. After stabilization, the first-month spacing of Part 4.4.1 sets the interval during the first month and one month sets it afterwards, a month running to the same date of the next month or the last day of a shorter month. The land disturbance occurring at any one time is judged over the whole project through September 30, 2026, an area counting as disturbed from each logged day through the day its stabilization is completed. A deadline at the close or end of a day is 23:59 that day, a number of days from a time ends at the same clock time that many days later, and a requirement met exactly at its deadline is on time.

Write /app/output/violations.csv with the header site_id,area_id,requirement,due,met and one row for each requirement due on or before 2026-09-30T23:59 that was not met by its deadline, so identical rows can occur. requirement is one of routine_inspection, storm_inspection, inspection_report, routine_maintenance, corrective_action, ca_log_identified, ca_log_completed, swppp_update, trackout_removal, stabilization_initiation and stabilization_completion. For an inspection report, area_id is the first area the inspection lists. due is the deadline and met the time it was met, as 2026-05-12T23:59 in local time, or none.
