The dialysis unit's pharmacy is auditing the vancomycin doses for 150 patients on maintenance hemodialysis who are being treated for MRSA infections. For every dialysis session, the pharmacy needs the predialysis vancomycin level that counts, how that level compares with the target range, and the dose the patient should receive at that session. The governing rules are the unit's policy in /app/data/protocol.md and the 2020 consensus guideline of ASHP, IDSA, PIDS and SIDP (Rybak et al., Am J Health-Syst Pharm 2020;77:835-864) to which it refers. No other rule or practice applies.

All of the input data is in /app/data/. The file /app/data/sessions.csv lists every dialysis session with its start and stop, the dialyzer's permeability to vancomycin (high or low), whether that session's vancomycin dose is given during the session (intradialytic) or after it ends (after_dialysis), and the patient's weight in kilograms before the session. The file /app/data/levels.csv holds every vancomycin result as the laboratory reported it, with the time the blood was drawn, in mg/L or umol/L (vancomycin, 1449.3 g/mol); a corrected result replaces the earlier result with the same accession, and a specimen that was rejected or cancelled has no result. Times are local New York time (America/New_York), to the minute.

Every patient started vancomycin at their first session in the data, which is their first session under the policy. Every session listed took place as recorded, no other session took place, and no patient missed a session. Every weight is accurate. The records are complete and correct.

Write your results to /app/output/session_doses.csv. The first line must be exactly patient_id,session_id,predialysis_level_mg_l,status,dose_mg and it must be followed by one row for each session, sorted by patient_id and then by session start. The predialysis_level_mg_l column is the session's predialysis level in mg/L with one decimal, or empty when there is none; status is none, below, within or above for that level against the target range; and dose_mg is the dose in mg for that session, or 0 when it is held. Levels are accepted within 0.05 mg/L, and status and dose_mg are compared exactly, so do not add extra columns, blank lines or explanations.
