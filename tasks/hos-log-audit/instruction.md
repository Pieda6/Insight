Prairie Line Freight and Mesa Regional Haul send their drivers' records of duty status to an outside compliance desk for a weekly hours-of-service audit. For every driver, the desk needs each point at which the driver drove when the hours-of-service rules did not allow it. The sole governing authority is 49 CFR Part 395 as in force on October 1, 2026; no other rules, guidance, state law or company policy applies.

All of the input data is in /app/data/. The file /app/data/carriers.json describes the two carriers, including whether each one operates commercial motor vehicles every day of the week, the time of day at which it begins its 24-hour period, and whether it keeps the time records that the short-haul exception requires. The file /app/data/terminals.json gives each terminal's city, its time zone and its offset from UTC, which stays the same for the whole period covered by the records. The file /app/data/drivers.json lists the drivers, giving each driver's carrier, home terminal and normal work reporting location. The file /app/data/logs.json holds each driver's record as a series of consecutive periods, each with a start and end time in UTC and one of four statuses: off_duty, sleeper_berth, driving or on_duty_not_driving.

Some periods carry notes. A driving period gives the greatest straight-line distance from the driver's normal work reporting location reached during that period, in statute miles. A driving period may also note adverse driving conditions and say whether they were known to the driver or the carrier beforehand. An on-duty period may note that the driver was traveling at the carrier's direction. The first period of a duty tour may note that the driver claims the exception in § 395.1(o). The off-duty period that follows a driver's work notes whether the driver was released at the normal work reporting location or somewhere else.

Every driver operates a property-carrying commercial motor vehicle in interstate commerce. The vehicle requires a commercial driver's license and is equipped with a sleeper berth, and each driver works only for the carrier listed. Apart from the provisions on adverse driving conditions, short-haul operations, sleeper berths, travel time and the exception in § 395.1(o), none of the exceptions in § 395.1 applies to any driver. Every driving period is driving time. Where a record notes adverse driving conditions that were not known beforehand, those conditions kept the driver from completing the run safely within the normal limits. Nothing before the first period in a driver's record counts toward any limit, and the records are complete and correct.

Write the result to /app/output/violations.csv. Its first line must be exactly driver_id,violation_start,rule. A stretch of driving is a run of consecutive driving periods with no other status between them. For each stretch, and for each rule that the driving in that stretch broke, write one row giving the driver's identifier, the start of the first minute of driving in that stretch that the rule did not permit, and the rule's name. Use 11_HOUR_DRIVING for the driving time limit in § 395.3(a)(3)(i), 14_HOUR_WINDOW for the limit in § 395.3(a)(2), 30_MINUTE_BREAK for § 395.3(a)(3)(ii) and 60_70_HOUR for § 395.3(b), in each case as changed by any exception that applies.

Write each time in UTC in the form 2026-09-21T20:40Z. Sort the rows by driver_id, then by violation_start, then by rule. A driver with no violations has no rows. The file is compared exactly, so it must not contain extra columns, blank lines, explanations or duplicate rows.
