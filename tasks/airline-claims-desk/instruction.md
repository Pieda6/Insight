Lakeshore Air's claims desk has a queue of passenger claims waiting to be settled. For every claim in that queue, the desk needs three answers: how much denied boarding compensation the passenger is owed, how much money must be refunded, and the date by which that refund has to be paid. Decide each claim under 14 CFR Part 250 and Part 260 as they were in force on October 1, 2026. No other law, guidance or policy applies. Lakeshore's contract of carriage gives passengers nothing beyond those two parts, and its only provision that matters here is the check-in deadline quoted in /app/data/carrier.json.

The records are in /app/data/. The file /app/data/claims.json holds one record per claim, giving the claim type and the ticket: when it was bought, how it was paid for, whether it is an award ticket bought with miles, its coupons with the flight, cabin, fare and reservation status of each, and, for each connection, whether the checked bags were tagged through and whether the passenger left the airport. It lists the paid extras, with each fee, its coupon and whether it was actually provided, and the checked bags, with each fee, whether that fee is linked to the bag by a unique identifier, and any bag fee subscription. Its event log records what happened and when: check-in, events at the gate, replacement transportation arranged, Lakeshore's notices of cancellations and schedule changes with what each offered, the passenger's replies, boarding, and baggage reports and deliveries. It also gives the moment the passenger asked for a refund, if they did.

The file /app/data/flights.json describes every flight: route, aircraft, designed seat count, any aircraft substitution, whether it is scheduled service, the lowest cash fare in each cabin, whether it operated or was cancelled, when its door opened at the gate, and its schedule history. Each schedule version gives departure and arrival times and the moment it was recorded, and it applies from that moment until the next version. The file /app/data/airports.json gives the city, country and time zone of each airport, /app/data/carrier.json gives the carrier's details, including its classes of service from highest to lowest, and /app/data/federal_holidays.json lists the observed federal holidays you should use.

Lakeshore Air is a U.S. air carrier that operates scheduled passenger service. It is the merchant of record for every ticket and fee in the queue, and no ticket agent was involved. Every itinerary was bought as one ticket in a single transaction, and Lakeshore operates every flight in the data. All money is in cents. Schedule and door times are local wall times at the airport concerned, and every other timestamp ends in Z and is in UTC. A coupon's fare includes all mandatory taxes and fees but no paid extras. When a passenger was denied boarding, the fare to the destination or first stopover is the sum of the coupon fares starting with the denied flight and running up to that point. A ticket is international, which is to say foreign air transportation, when any of its flights touches an airport outside the United States, and every other ticket is domestic.

A passenger's original itinerary is the ticketed flights as they were scheduled when the ticket was bought, and an itinerary offered in a notice is the listed flights as they were scheduled when the notice was sent. A flight departs at its scheduled time, and an itinerary departs when its first flight does. The duration of a flight is its scheduled duration. Passengers are given the opportunity to deplane when the aircraft door opens at the gate. No passenger in the queue is an individual with a disability. A passenger who declines a change that is not significant has chosen not to travel, so an extra booked for that trip that went unused was not provided because of the passenger's own choice. A passenger who turns down or does not answer the alternatives offered for a cancelled or significantly changed flight uses no part of that ticket. A flight counts as cancelled from the moment Lakeshore notifies the passenger. Every date, including the day on which a flight departs, is a calendar date in UTC, and the day from which a refund due date is counted is not itself included in the count. The records are complete and correct.
