A company needs to ship a batch of heavy freight on pallets using its own fleet of tractor-trailers, which we call units. Trucks cost money to run, so the company wants to move every pallet using as few units as it can. Every unit that leaves the yard loaded has to be legal to drive on the Interstate under the federal axle weight rules in 23 CFR 658.17. Your job is to produce a load plan that does this.

The fleet is described in /app/data/units.csv. Each row is one unit and gives its unit_id, its tractor_model, the positions of the steer axle and the two drive axles (steer_x_in, drive1_x_in, drive2_x_in), the position of the kingpin (kingpin_x_in), and the usable inside length of the trailer (trailer_inside_length_in). Each trailer has a sliding rear tandem that can be locked in one of several positions, and the allowed settings for each unit are listed in /app/data/sliders.csv. For every setting that file gives the positions of the two trailer axles and the empty weight that the unit puts on each of its five axles when it carries no freight. The freight itself is in /app/data/pallets.csv, which gives each pallet's pallet_id, a description, its weight_lb and its length_in. Two worked examples showing how the numbers fit together are in /app/reference/worked_examples.md, and you should read them before you start.

All of the positions ending in _x_in are measured in inches from the inside face of the trailer's front wall, with rearward counting as positive. The steer axle sits ahead of that wall, so its position is negative. A pallet's position is the distance in whole inches from the front wall to the front edge of that pallet. Pallets are loaded in a single lane, one behind another, and each one must sit entirely inside the trailer's inside length. Pallets cannot overlap, but they can go in any order and you are free to leave gaps between them. Treat each pallet's weight as acting at the middle of its length. Whatever load ends up on a tandem is shared equally by its two axles, for both the drive tandem and the trailer tandem. Every unit you use must be given exactly one slider setting, and it has to be one of the settings listed for that particular unit.

The axles are numbered from the front: axle 1 is the steer axle, axles 2 and 3 are the drive tandem, and axles 4 and 5 are the trailer tandem. The steer axle is a single axle and may carry at most 20,000 lb. Each tandem may carry at most 34,000 lb in total. The gross weight of the loaded unit, meaning the sum over all five axles, may not exceed 80,000 lb. On top of these limits, the Bridge Formula must be satisfied for every group of two or more consecutive axles. For a group of N axles whose outer axles are L feet apart, the total weight on that group may not exceed W = 500 times (LN / (N - 1) + 12N + 36) pounds. To get L, take the distance in inches between the first and last axle in the group, convert it to feet, and round to the nearest whole foot, with exactly half a foot rounding up. Then round W to the nearest 500 lb, with an exact 250 rounding up. There is one exception you must apply: the group made of axles 2 through 5 may carry 34,000 lb on each of its two tandems (68,000 lb in total) even where the formula gives less, provided the distance from axle 2 to axle 5 is 36 feet or more once rounded as above. When checking any of these limits, use the exact axle loads, not rounded ones.

The goal is to use the smallest number of units that can legally carry every pallet. Any plan that is legal and uses that minimum number will be accepted, so you do not need to match a particular arrangement. A plan that uses more units than necessary, leaves a pallet out, or breaks any of the rules above will fail.

Write three files. The first is /app/output/load_plan.csv with the header pallet_id,unit_id,position_in and exactly one row for every pallet, saying which unit it goes on and where its front edge sits. The second is /app/output/units.csv with the header unit_id,slider_position_id and one row for each unit you actually use, naming the slider setting chosen for it. Do not list units that carry nothing. The third is /app/output/summary.json, a JSON object with two keys. The key units_used holds the number of units used as a whole number. The key axle_loads_lb maps each used unit_id to a list of five whole numbers giving the loaded weight on each axle in the order steer, drive 1, drive 2, trailer 1, trailer 2. Round these to the nearest pound with halves rounding up. Values within 1 lb of the exact loads implied by your plan will be accepted.
