Caribe Water Resources Engineering is reviewing how the sediment basins on twelve construction sites in Puerto Rico performed during the 2026 wet season, from April 1 through September 30. For every basin cell and storm the owners want the peak inflow and outflow, the highest water surface and when it occurred, and whether and how much water went over the emergency spillway. The hydrology follows NRCS NEH Part 630 Chapters 10 (July 2004), 16 (March 2007) and 17 (April 2014) and TR-55 Chapter 3 (June 1986); the hydraulics follow FHWA HEC-22, 4th edition (February 2024), Sections 10.3 and 10.5.

The data is in /app/data/. sites.json gives each site's 2-year, 24-hour rainfall, its drainage areas and its basin cells. Each drainage area has its acres, soil group, curve numbers (TR-55 Table 2-2a) and TR-55 Table 3-1 sheet-flow surface for the undisturbed, disturbed and stabilized states, its flow path (sheet flow, paved or unpaved shallow concentrated flow and sometimes a channel with flow area, wetted perimeter and Manning's n; lengths in feet, slopes in feet per foot) and the cell it drains to. A cell, main or forebay, is a trapezoid (bottom elevation, length and width, side slope in horizontal feet per vertical foot) or surveyed contours (elevation and enclosed area in square feet). A main cell has a circular dewatering orifice (diameter in inches), a circular riser open at the top and a broad-crested emergency spillway, and discharges_to names the cell receiving its outflow, or is null. A forebay drains into its main cell through a divider made of a sharp-crested weir (crest elevation, length, coefficient) and a circular orifice. rain_gauge.json holds each site's 6-minute gauge record (interval start in local time, rain in inches); intervals without measurable rain are not listed. area_states.json gives the date from which each area was in each state, from the start of that date.

Coefficients are dimensionless, as in HEC-22 equations 10.23 and 10.32. Main-cell outlets discharge freely and their outlet pipes never limit the flow. The dewatering orifice passes nothing while the water is below its centroid. Flow through a divider runs from the higher water surface to the lower, the lower pool acting as tailwater on the weir and the orifice as HEC-22 Section 10.3.3 describes. The cells were dry at the start of April 1, gain or lose water only through runoff, outlets and dividers, and never reached an embankment top. Rain that follows six hours or more without measurable rain starts a new storm. Its runoff comes from the rain accumulated since it began, as in Chapter 10 example 10-6, at the average antecedent runoff condition, and each area uses the curve number and sheet-flow surface of its state when the storm begins, its time of concentration following TR-55 Chapter 3 including the minimum it sets. Each drainage area is its own hydrologic unit. The office's model uses the 6-minute gauge interval as the unit duration and the routing time step, reads unit hydrograph ordinates from the discharge ratios of Chapter 16 Table 16-1 by linear interpolation, starts the response to each 6-minute increment of runoff at the beginning of that increment, and adds the hydrographs reaching a cell at each time point. Contour storage uses the conic formula of HEC-22 equation 10.19, with the area at a level between two contours interpolated linearly. Every flow in and out of every cell, including the flow through a divider and the outflow of one main cell into another at the same time point, enters the storage-indication equation as the average of its values at the two ends of each step, and every step is solved on the continuous stage-storage and stage-discharge relations, continuously through the season.
