Caribe Water Resources Engineering is reviewing how the sediment basins on twelve construction sites in Puerto Rico performed during the 2026 wet season, from April 1 through September 30. The owners want, for every basin and every storm, the peak flow into the basin, the peak flow out of it, the highest water surface elevation it reached and whether its emergency spillway flowed. The hydrology follows the NRCS National Engineering Handbook Part 630: the curve number runoff method of Chapter 10 (July 2004), the dimensionless unit hydrograph of Chapter 16 (March 2007) and the storage-indication reservoir routing of Chapter 17 (April 2014). The basin hydraulics follow FHWA HEC-22, 4th edition (February 2024), Section 10.3 for stage-storage and stage-discharge relations and Section 10.5 for storage routing.

The data is in /app/data/. sites.json describes each site's drainage areas and basins. Each drainage area has its size in acres, its time of concentration in hours, its hydrologic soil group, its curve numbers for the undisturbed, disturbed and stabilized states (taken from TR-55 Table 2-2a), and the basin it drains to. Each basin is trapezoidal, with its bottom elevation, bottom length and width in feet, side slope as horizontal feet per vertical foot, and embankment top. Its outlets are a circular dewatering orifice (diameter in inches, invert elevation and discharge coefficient), a circular riser open at the top (diameter in feet, crest elevation, weir coefficient and orifice coefficient) and a broad-crested emergency spillway (crest elevation, crest length and weir coefficient). discharges_to names the basin that receives its outflow, or is null. Elevations are in feet. rain_gauge.json holds each site's rain gauge record in 6-minute intervals, with the start of each interval in local time and the rain in inches; intervals without measurable rain are not listed. area_states.json gives the date from which each drainage area was in each state; a state applies from the start of its date.

The weir and orifice coefficients are dimensionless, as in HEC-22 equations 10.23 and 10.32. Every outlet discharges freely: no tailwater reaches it and the outlet pipe never limits the flow. The dewatering orifice passes nothing while the water is below its centroid. The basins were dry at the start of April 1, nothing enters or leaves them other than the drainage area runoff, upstream basin outflow and the outlets, and the water never reached an embankment top. Each drainage area is its own hydrologic unit. A storm is the rain from a measurable interval until six hours pass without measurable rain, its runoff comes from the rain accumulated since it began, as in Chapter 10 example 10-6, at the average antecedent runoff condition, and each area uses the curve number of its state when the storm begins. The office's model uses the 6-minute gauge interval as the unit duration of the unit hydrographs and as the routing time step, reads unit hydrograph ordinates from the discharge ratios of Chapter 16 Table 16-1 by linear interpolation, starts the response to each 6-minute increment of runoff at the beginning of that increment, adds the hydrographs reaching a basin at each time point, and routes every basin continuously through the season, solving each routing step on the continuous stage-storage and stage-discharge relations rather than on a tabulated working curve. An upstream basin's outflow reaches the basin below it at the same time point.

Write /app/output/basin_storms.csv with the header site_id,basin_id,storm_start,peak_inflow_cfs,peak_outflow_cfs,peak_stage_ft,spillway_flow and one row for each basin and each storm at its site, sorted by site_id, basin_id and storm_start. storm_start is the start of the storm's first measurable interval, as 2026-04-14T14:00. The peaks are the largest values at the 6-minute time points from storm_start up to the start of the site's next storm, or through the end of September 30 for the last storm. Flows are in cubic feet per second and peak_stage_ft is the water surface elevation in feet, each with three decimals. spillway_flow is yes when the water rose above the emergency spillway crest during that period and no otherwise. Flows are accepted within 0.5 percent or 0.01 cubic feet per second and stages within 0.005 feet; storm_start and spillway_flow must match exactly.
